## The shape of an urge

Urges have a shape. They build, they peak, and they fade, often within minutes. While you are inside one, it feels like the climb will never stop. That feeling is part of the urge, not a forecast.

## What urge surfing asks you to do

The idea comes from relapse-prevention research, especially a program called mindfulness-based relapse prevention. Instead of fighting the urge or obeying it, you watch it:

- Notice it. Name it plainly: "This is an urge."
- Locate it. Where do you feel it, and how strong is it from 1 to 10?
- Ride it. Breathe, and keep watching the number. Let it rise without acting.
- Let it pass. Check the number again in a few minutes. It usually drops.

## What the research says

In trials with people recovering from substance use, programs that teach this skill reduced relapse compared with usual care, and some benefits lasted months. A smaller study with smokers found that brief urge-surfing instructions weakened the link between cravings and smoking.

These studies weren't about this habit, so treat the evidence as a reasonable guide rather than a guarantee.

## How Go a round uses it

Go a round is urge surfing with a clock. You rate the urge, play three minutes, and rate it again. The before-and-after numbers in your log are your own evidence of the drop.
