## The claim being checked

> "Testosterone peaks on day 7, so abstaining makes you stronger."

This line travels across forums and short videos, usually with a chart and no citation. It is the claim this article checks. It is labeled a myth because the evidence behind it does not hold up.

## Where it comes from

Almost every version traces back to one paper published in 2003 in the *Journal of Zhejiang University Science*. Researchers measured blood testosterone in 28 men over several days of abstinence. They reported a short rise around day 7, after which levels went back to where they started.

Even taken at face value, that is a brief bump in one blood measure in a small group. It says nothing about strength, energy or anything a person would notice.

## Why it no longer counts

The paper was later retracted. A retraction means the journal withdrew it from the scientific record; in this case the notice cited duplication. A retracted study cannot support a claim, however often it is repeated.

No study since has shown a lasting hormonal change from a month of abstinence, let alone a benefit that follows from one.

## What this means for your 30 days

You don't need a hormone story to do this. People take on challenges like this for focus, for discipline, for their own reasons. Those are good enough. If someone sells you a physical payoff, ask for the source. Usually it is this paper.
