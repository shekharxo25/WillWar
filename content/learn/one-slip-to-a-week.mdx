## The pattern

You reset. A voice says, "That's it, the whole thing's blown, might as well." One reset becomes three days, then a week.

Researchers call this the abstinence violation effect. It was described by Alan Marlatt and colleagues, who studied why people return to old habits after a single lapse.

## What drives it

Two things make it worse:

- Treating the lapse as proof about who you are, rather than as one event with causes.
- All-or-nothing rules, where any lapse means the whole effort counts for nothing.

In a study of people quitting smoking, those who blamed a lapse on something fixed about themselves were more likely to return to smoking fully.

## How to stop it

- Reset fast. Tap Reset streak and move on. The longer you avoid it, the louder the story gets.
- Keep the record. Your best streak and your tally stay on the wall. One reset doesn't erase the days you kept.
- Name the cause, not the person. Late night? Bored? Scrolling? That's information for tonight, not a verdict.
- Change one thing. Pick a single adjustment, like the phone out of the bedroom, and go again.

## A note on the evidence

This comes mostly from substance-use research. The idea is well established there and makes sense here, but it hasn't been tested on this habit directly.
