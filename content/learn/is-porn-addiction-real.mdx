## Two camps

Some researchers and clinicians describe problematic use as an addiction, similar to gambling. Others argue it fits better as a compulsive behaviour, or as a symptom of something else, such as anxiety, loneliness or stress. Critics of the addiction model point to inconsistent evidence for the brain changes it predicts.

The World Health Organization took a middle path. ICD-11 doesn't list "porn addiction". It lists compulsive sexual behaviour disorder, a broader diagnosis about loss of control and harm.

## Moral incongruence

One of the more consistent findings is about values. A meta-analysis by Joshua Grubbs and colleagues found that people who morally disapprove of something they do are much more likely to describe themselves as addicted, even when how often they do it is similar to others.

That doesn't mean their struggle isn't real. It means the label "addict" can come from the gap between what you value and what you do, as much as from the behaviour itself.

## Why the label matters less than you think

Whatever you call it, the useful questions are the same:

- Do you feel in control of it?
- Is it causing distress or harming your work, relationships or health?
- Have repeated efforts to change failed?

If the answers worry you, a clinician can help sort out what's going on, without you needing to settle the debate first.
