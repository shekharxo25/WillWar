## What the study did

The Health Professionals Follow-up Study asked about 32,000 men in the United States how often they ejaculated in different decades of life, then followed them for up to 18 years. Researchers compared those reports with later prostate cancer diagnoses.

## What it found

Men who reported ejaculating more often in their twenties and forties had a somewhat lower rate of prostate cancer later on, compared with men who reported it less often. The finding held after the researchers adjusted for several other factors.

## What it doesn't show

This is an association. People who differ in one habit often differ in others, and a study like this can't fully separate them. It also relied on memory, which is imperfect.

It does not show that a month-long challenge raises anyone's risk. The study looked at patterns over years, not a few weeks. And it certainly doesn't support the opposite claim you may have seen online, that abstaining protects your health. There's no good evidence for that.

## The practical bit

A 30-day challenge is a test of routine and attention, not a health intervention in either direction. If you notice symptoms such as pain, trouble urinating or blood in urine or semen, see a doctor. That's true whatever challenge you are doing.
