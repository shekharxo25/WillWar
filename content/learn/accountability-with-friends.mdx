## What helps

A large meta-analysis found that monitoring your progress makes you more likely to reach a goal, and that the effect was bigger when progress was physically recorded or reported to someone else. Committing in advance can help too: in a field study, smokers who signed a commitment contract were more likely to pass a test months later.

## What can backfire

Accountability turns sour when it becomes surveillance or shame. If a lapse means public embarrassment, people hide it, stop checking in, or quit. The research here is thinner, but the pattern is familiar to anyone who has dropped out of a group chat.

## How camps are built

Camps keep the useful part and leave out the rest:

- Handles, not names. Your camp sees your number, never the reason it reset.
- No reset feed. Nobody is notified when you reset. Your count just starts again.
- Preset nudges only. Five short messages like "I'm in your corner". No free-text messages, so no lectures.
- Paused, not punished. Miss two days and your row shows "Paused" until you check in again.
- Leave or mute any time. No explanation needed.

## The honest grade

Mixed. Tracking and gentle accountability help many people. Pressure helps fewer. Use a camp if it makes you feel backed up. If it makes you feel watched, leave it; your streak is still yours.
