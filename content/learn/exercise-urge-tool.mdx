## What the research found

In smoking research, people who did a short bout of exercise reported weaker cravings than people who sat still, and the difference lasted for a while afterwards. A meta-analysis that pooled individual data from many studies found the same thing. Even moderate activity, like a brisk walk, was enough.

## Why it might help

Movement changes what you are paying attention to, gets you out of the room where the urge started, and gives your hands and body a job. Those are all things that make it easier to let an urge pass.

## The caveat

These studies were about cigarettes and other substances. It is reasonable to expect something similar here, but it hasn't been tested directly. Treat it as a tool worth trying, not a promise.

## How to use it

- When an urge builds, stand up first. Decisions are easier on your feet.
- 20 squats, a set of push-ups, or a walk around the block. Ten minutes is plenty.
- Go outside if you can. A change of room helps on its own.

After a round, if the urge is still at 8 or higher, the app will suggest exactly this.
