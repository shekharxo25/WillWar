## The claims being checked

> "After 30 days you get superpowers."
>
> "You develop an aura. People are drawn to you."
>
> "Retention makes you magnetic and more attractive."

These are the claims this article checks, quoted as they commonly appear. Each is labeled a myth.

## What we found

We found no study that tests these claims and supports them. They spread through testimonials, not research.

When a physical mechanism is offered, it is usually the day-7 hormone claim. That traces to a single 2003 paper that was later retracted. See "The day-7 testosterone claim, traced to its source".

Research on online abstinence communities has looked at why people join and how they feel. It describes what members believe and report. It doesn't show that the claimed effects happen.

## What people may genuinely notice

Some people do say they feel sharper or more confident during a challenge. That is worth taking seriously as personal experience, with ordinary explanations:

- More time, because fewer late nights online.
- Better routines, because the challenge makes you plan your evenings.
- Pride, because keeping a promise to yourself feels good.

Those are real and worth having. They are not superpowers.

## Why we care

Overblown promises set people up to feel cheated, or to blame themselves when nothing magical happens. Do the 30 days for reasons that hold up. Yours are good enough.
