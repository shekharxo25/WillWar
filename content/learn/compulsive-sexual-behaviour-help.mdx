## It is a recognised condition

In 2019 the World Health Organization added compulsive sexual behaviour disorder to its classification of diseases, ICD-11, under code 6C72. That matters: it means clinicians have shared criteria, and help exists.

## What the criteria describe

The diagnosis is about a pattern, over six months or more, of failing to control intense, repetitive sexual urges or behaviour, where:

- the behaviour has become a central focus, at the expense of health, responsibilities or other interests,
- repeated efforts to cut down haven't worked,
- it continues despite clear harm or little satisfaction, and
- it causes marked distress or problems in relationships, work or daily life.

The WHO is explicit that distress coming only from moral judgment or disapproval doesn't, by itself, meet the criteria.

## Signs it's time to talk to someone

- You have tried many times to change and can't, and it is affecting your work, studies or relationships.
- It's taking up hours of your day.
- You feel hopeless, or you are using it to cope with something painful.
- You have thoughts of harming yourself.

## Who to talk to

A doctor, a psychologist or a counsellor is a good start. In India, Tele-MANAS is free and open 24/7 at 14416. If you are in immediate danger, call your local emergency number. The Support page lists more options.

A challenge app is not treatment. If this sounds like you, getting real help is the strongest move you can make.
